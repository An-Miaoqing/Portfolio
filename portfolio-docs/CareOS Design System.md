# **Rule 6 — Motion has meaning**

Animation shouldn’t exist just because it looks nice.

Every motion should answer a question.

For example:

Lines growing together.

Meaning:

These domains are connected.

Cards expanding.

Meaning:

We’re looking deeper.

Zooming out.

Meaning:

We’re returning to the bigger picture.

A split animation.

Meaning:

The workflow branches.

Merge animation.

Meaning:

Two paths rejoin.

If an animation has no meaning, it shouldn’t exist.

The screenshot proves the idea.

It doesn’t introduce it.

# **Rule 10 — Nothing decorative**

Everything on the page should answer one of these questions:

- Does it explain?
- Does it guide?
- Does it reinforce?
- Does it provide evidence?

If the answer is no, remove it.

That includes icons, animations, gradients, and effects.

# **Then comes something I think you’ll appreciate**

Looking back over everything we’ve worked on over the past year—not just CareOS, but also your portfolio, system models, and documentation—I notice a recurring pattern.

You consistently think in **layers**:

- Business
- Process
- Data
- System
- Implementation

I wouldn’t hide that.

Instead of visitors simply seeing a polished website, they experience the same layered reasoning that you use to analyze and design systems.

Every major concept should be explainable at five levels:

|---|---|

|**System**|Which components collaborate?|
|**Implementation**|How is it built?|

Imagine clicking on **Bookings** and being able to move through those five lenses. That wouldn’t just showcase CareOS—it would showcase _how you think_.
